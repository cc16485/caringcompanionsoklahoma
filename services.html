Services

What our companion caregivers do.

Companion care is for older adults who can still take care of themselves physically but need company, a hand around the house, or a ride. Here's everything a visit can include.

Every visit is part of The Good Day Club, our way of helping older adults stay independent longer.

Companionship

Conversation, cards and puzzles, a walk to the mailbox, looking through old photos together. Someone who shows up, every time, and is glad to be there.

Light housekeeping

Dishes, laundry, tidying the rooms they use, changing bed linens, taking out the trash. The house stays the way they like it.

Meal preparation

Planning and cooking meals they enjoy, following any diet their doctor has given them, and sitting down to eat together.

Errands and shopping

Groceries, the pharmacy pickup, the post office. Done for them, or with them if they'd like to get out.

Rides to appointments

A caregiver drives them to the doctor, the salon, church or lunch with a friend, waits, and brings them home.

Seated exercise

A guided routine done sitting down, to keep strength, balance and energy up. Your caregiver leads it and does it right alongside them.

Medication reminders

A reminder at the right time, every visit, so medications get taken on schedule. Reminders only; caregivers don't handle or set up medications.

Respite for family

Time off for the family member who does most of the caregiving. Go to work, run errands or rest, knowing someone is there.

Add-on service

HomeTogether TV

Family video calls right on their TV, plus camera-free sensors for gentle, passive safety monitoring. It keeps everyone connected on the days between visits, without taking away privacy or independence.

Good to know

What companion care doesn't include.

The license we're applying for from the Oklahoma State Department of Health is for companion services. That means our caregivers don't give hands-on help with the body. They will not help with:

  • Bathing, showering or help getting dressed
  • Toileting or incontinence care
  • Lifting, transfers or help getting in and out of bed
  • Handling, sorting or giving medications
  • Any nursing or medical care

If those needs come up, we'll tell you honestly and talk with you about what kind of care would fit.
