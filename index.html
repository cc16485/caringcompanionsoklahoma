What a visit can look like

Help with the everyday, and good company while it's done.

Every visit is planned around the person: their routines, the things they enjoy, and the jobs that have gotten harder.

Companionship

Conversation, cards and puzzles, a walk to the mailbox, looking through old photos together. Someone who shows up, every time, and is glad to be there.

Light housekeeping

Dishes, laundry, tidying the rooms they use, changing bed linens, taking out the trash. The house stays the way they like it.

Meal preparation

Planning and cooking meals they enjoy, following any diet their doctor has given them, and sitting down to eat together.

Errands and shopping

Groceries, the pharmacy pickup, the post office. Done for them, or with them if they'd like to get out.

Rides to appointments

A caregiver drives them to the doctor, the salon, church or lunch with a friend, waits, and brings them home.

Seated exercise

A guided routine done sitting down, to keep strength, balance and energy up. Your caregiver leads it and does it right alongside them.

Honest from the first call

Companion care, not personal care.

The Oklahoma license we're applying for is for companion services, so it's important you know exactly what that covers before you call.

What our caregivers do

  • Companionship
  • Light housekeeping
  • Meal preparation
  • Errands and shopping
  • Rides to appointments
  • Seated exercise
  • Medication reminders
  • Respite for family

What they don't do

  • Bathing, showering or help getting dressed
  • Toileting or incontinence care
  • Lifting, transfers or help getting in and out of bed
  • Handling, sorting or giving medications
  • Any nursing or medical care

If your loved one needs hands-on help, we'll tell you so at the first visit rather than take on care we can't give.

Getting started

Four steps from first call to first visit.

  1. Call or book online

    Tell us what's going on. We'll ask a few questions and find a time to come out. We're not open yet: visits begin once we're licensed, planned for June 2027.

  2. Free visit at home

    We meet your loved one, see the home, and learn their routines and what matters to them.

  3. A written plan

    Together we write down the help, the days and the hours. We review it with you at least every six months.

  4. Care begins

    Your caregiver arrives knowing the plan. You can call the office any weekday with questions or changes.
